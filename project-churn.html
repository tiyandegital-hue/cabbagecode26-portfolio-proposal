Why customers leave — and who can be saved.

A subscription business wanted to know where churn was really coming from. The answer wasn't "more discounts" — it was the billing plan itself.

The question

Which customers are leaving, is it the product or the plan, and what's the cheapest lever to pull first?

The data

A synthetic sample of 1,800 subscription customers over six months: plan type, tenure, spend, support contacts, churn flag.

The method

Cohort churn curves by plan, then a logistic model on tenure + support contacts to score at-risk accounts. Metric reported: churn rate by segment — the number the business actually manages.

The result

Monthly-plan customers churn at 2.3× the annual rate. The model flags the top 10% at-risk accounts; in the sample they hold 41% of next-quarter churn.

Caveats — stated upfront

  • Synthetic sample data. No real customers, no real churn — the method is the deliverable being demonstrated.
  • Correlation, not causation: annual plans may attract more committed customers rather than cause commitment.
  • On real data this ships with a cleaning log, model diagnostics, and a plain-language readout for non-technical stakeholders.
