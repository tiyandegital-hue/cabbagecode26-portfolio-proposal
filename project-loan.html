Case study · Excel + SQL

Score the risk, not the applicant.

A lender needed a risk view a loan officer could explain in one sentence. Three bands, six features, zero black box.

Why this metric, not accuracy With a 4.3% base default rate, a model that predicts "no default" for everyone scores 95.7% accuracy — and tells you nothing. Default rate per band is the number that actually prices risk. Report the metric that fits the problem, and say why.

The question

Can simple, explainable bands separate loans that repay from loans that default — well enough to price on?

The data

Synthetic sample of 2,400 loan records: amount, term, income band, employment length, past delinquencies, outcome.

The method

Weighted scorecard in Excel, cutoffs set on a training split, validated on held-out sample data. SQL does the extraction and the band assignment at scale.

The result

High-risk band defaults at 18.4% vs 2.1% for low-risk — an 8.8× separation a loan officer can quote.

Sample  Illustrative default rates on synthetic data. The shape — wide separation, monotonic bands — is what validation looks for.

Caveats — stated upfront

  • Synthetic sample data — no real borrowers, no real defaults.
  • Risk models affect people's access to credit. A production version needs fairness review, monitoring, and a human in the loop — this demo is a methods illustration, not a lending product.
  • Band cutoffs here are illustrative; real cutoffs are set with the lender's risk team, not by the analyst alone.
